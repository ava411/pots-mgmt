Privacy: How this technology accesses other data

The technology or app requests access to other device data or applications, such as your phone’s camera, photos, or contacts
Yes, only with your permission. It connects to...
  • Other: Apple Health, in the iPhone app, to read sleep, resting heart rate, HRV, steps, workouts and periods

Here is how you can check your settings, including permissions set as a default… The app reads nothing from Apple Health until you allow it. To change what it reads, on your iPhone open Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Health, then POTS Tracker.

The technology or app allows you to share the collected data with your social media accounts, like Facebook
Yes, only with your permission.

Here is how you can check your settings… Your data leaves the app only when you save a backup or a spreadsheet, in Settings under Data, and choose where it goes.

User Options: What you can do with the data that we collect

The technology or app allows you to access, edit, share, or delete the data we have about you
Yes. You can…
  • Access your data
  • Edit your data
  • Share your data
  • Delete your data

Here is how to do this… Review shows your log, and Today has “Edit today’s check-in”. Settings, under Data, saves a backup, downloads a spreadsheet, or deletes everything. A backup you saved stays until you delete it. The iPhone app keeps your data out of iCloud and computer backups; in a web browser, that depends on the browser.

Deleted immediately

POTS Tracker complies with all applicable laws regarding breaches. We keep none of your data, so there is none on our systems to disclose.

Definitions

Health data can include, but is not limited to: wellness information (e.g., exercise or fitness habits, nutrition, or sleep data), health markers (e.g., blood pressure, BMI, or glucose), information on physical or mental health conditions, insurance or health care information, or information that integrates into or receives information from a personal health record.

Identifiable data means: data, such as your name, phone number, email, address, health services, information on your physical or mental health conditions, or your social security number, that can be used on its own or with other information to identify you.

Data broker means: companies that collect personal information about consumers from a variety of public and non-public sources and resell the information to other companies (From FTC: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2012/12/ftc-study-data-broker-industrys-collection-use-consumer-data).

Encryption means: a method of converting an original message of regular text into encoded text in such a way that only authorized parties can read it.

Cloud computing means: a kind of Internet-based computing that provides shared processing resources and data to computers and other devices on demand.

Deactivation means: an individual takes action or a company ceases operation or deactivates an individual’s account due to inactivity.

Breach means: an unauthorized disclosure.
